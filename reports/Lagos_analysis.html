Rank average. Rank each LGA separately within each component, use average ranks for ties, then average six ranks per side. Easy to explain; discards differences in magnitude. Demand > 9.333333; supply readiness < 8.500000.

Mean percentages. Average six original percentages per side. Retains percentage-point differences; components with wider variation can contribute more to differences between LGAs. Demand > 34.641203; supply readiness < 49.760370.

Median/IQR. For each component, subtract its LGA median and divide by its IQR, then average six values per side. Measures distance from typical conditions using a robust spread. Demand > -0.002326; supply readiness < -0.004110.

Median count. Count demand components above their own medians and supply components below theirs. Flag a side at 4 or more of 6. This is a coarse majority rule; it does not use a final-score median.

Z-score average · main approach. For each component, subtract the mean across the 17 LGAs and divide by its sample standard deviation; average six values per side. Retains differences in magnitude on a common variability scale. Demand > 0.001456; supply readiness < -0.131082.

For the four continuous approaches, each side is flagged relative to the median of its own final-score distribution: higher demand, lower supply readiness. Exact median ties are not flagged. Normalization and cutoffs give each of the 17 LGAs equal contribution; these are relative thresholds, not externally validated service standards. Each component receives one-sixth weight. Median count instead uses its explicit 4-of-6 rule.

04 · ADJUSTED ASSOCIATIONS

Modern contraceptive use, individual demand and local supply risk

Woman-level logistic regression · FPN2 = 1

Outcome: current modern contraceptive use. Confirmed nonusers and traditional-only users are coded 0; confirmed modern users are coded 1. Unresolved use or method classifications are excluded from models.

All models adjust for: age and age squared, education, partnership status, fertility goal and urban/rural residence. Original SURGO woman weights · 17 LGAs · no random effects or interactions.

Demand risk score: each woman's average standardized concern score.Supply risk score: −1 × the matched LGA–rural/urban supply-readiness score.Higher values on both axes mean less favorable conditions.

Demand risk score · personal responses. These main models use an equal-weight average of z-scores, not PCA. The six inputs are healthcare permission barriers, anticipated social stigma, health/side-effect beliefs, future-fertility concerns, low method confidence (displayed as “Low method knowledge”) and opposition to FP. Each is coded 1 = concern, 0 = no concern. For each item, subtract its SURGO-weighted mean among valid FPN2 = 1 respondents and divide by its weighted population SD. Average the woman's six standardized responses; all six are required. The same formula applies wherever she lives. This gives 62 distinct personal scores among 1,865 modeled women. Identical response patterns receive identical scores. The LGA planning demand index is separate and is not used in these models.

Supply risk score · assigned service environment. Start with the six facility summaries assigned by her exact LGA and rural/urban status: short + long-acting offering, free-product provision, trained-provider share, counselor availability, three-LARC stock breadth and most-popular-product stock. Rural women receive rural summaries and urban women urban summaries within the same LGA. Standardize each profile measure using that component's fixed mean and sample SD across the 17 LGA-wide planning summaries; average the six standardized values and multiply by −1. Because all six raw supply measures run higher = stronger provision, this reversal makes higher supply risk = weaker recorded provision. Women in the same profile share a supply risk score. There are 18 observed profiles; inference remains clustered across 17 LGAs.

Both scores enter continuously in their original average-z units, with no median split or final-index restandardization. They are not percentages or 0–100 scales, and zero does not mean no barriers. PCA-weighted alternatives are presented separately in the appendix.

Approved component fallback: Ibeju-Lekki–Urban has no known most-popular-product stock status. Only that component uses the LGA value; its other five components remain Urban-specific. This is flagged for 62 women in need, of whom 51 are complete cases in these models. The original missing stock-status value is preserved.

Score construction, model tests and interpretation

The two tests in the both-score model each test whether that score's coefficient is zero, conditional on the other score and covariates. Holm correction is applied across these two tests only. They do not test whether demand matters more than supply or attribute a percentage of nonuse.

An odds ratio is a change in odds, not a percentage-point change in use. The curves translate the fitted coefficients into weighted average probabilities. Each score is varied over its observed range while other predictors retain their observed values; profiles need not be jointly observed and are not intervention forecasts.

The individual score uses six binary inputs, so at most 64 response patterns are possible; it need not have a unique value for every woman or the same number of values as supply risk. Standardization uses the weighted mean and weighted population standard deviation within the supplied need group, with item-specific valid denominators. Missing responses are not coded zero. These six equally weighted standardized concerns form a planning-oriented composite, not a validated psychological scale. Equal standardized weights do not imply equal raw-percentage or intervention effects.

All five models converged, had full-rank designs and finite coefficients. Maximum VIF was 3.94. Weighted logistic coefficients match the corresponding svyglm point estimates to numerical precision. Leaving out one LGA at a time preserves the negative coefficient signs for the demand index, opposition and low confidence. The near-zero joint supply-risk coefficient changes sign across deletions, reinforcing its uncertain interpretation.

Uncertainty: 95% coefficient intervals use LGA-clustered CR2 covariance and coefficient-specific Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. The supply-risk terms have approximately 2.6 effective degrees of freedom, reflecting concentrated geographic leverage despite the woman-level sample size. The Ibeju-Lekki–Urban facility profile is based on one facility; its component fallback and profile counts are downloadable. LGA clustering uses a known geographic group, not a verified official survey PSU. Official EA/stratum fields are unconfirmed; full complex-survey design inference cannot be claimed. The Senegal source scripts used weights-only estimation. These models add geographic clustering, and contain no random effects.

What has not been established: no formal power analysis has been conducted. There are still only 17 LGA contexts, even though the regressions have woman-level rows. Intervals condition on the estimated scores; their construction uncertainty has not been propagated. Cross-sectional associations cannot establish causal binding constraints, demand-versus-supply contribution percentages, or that a nonsignificant supply score means supply is adequate.

Methods: CR2 covariance for weighted GLMs; small-sample block tests; survey-weighted GLMs.

Weights, uncertainty and reproducibility

All SURGO point estimates use the original final weight field. The need subgroup is selected after constructing the positive-weight survey frame. Official enumeration-area/PSU and stratum identifiers remain unconfirmed in the available files, so the overview bars withhold full-design confidence intervals. The model sections use separately labeled geographic LGA-clustered inference, which does not establish the official survey design. Household-ID digits and selection probabilities are not treated as cluster identifiers. The analysis script accepts explicitly confirmed PSU/stratum fields when they become available and then calculates Taylor-linearized logit intervals; no finite-population correction is assumed.

Facility summaries are unweighted because facility sampling weights were not supplied. Each component uses its valid denominator. Most-popular-product stock coverage can be low; the denominator download should accompany interpretation. The only free-provision exception is the one previously authorized Urban facility in Ibeju-Lekki. Original input files and the previous summary HTML remain unchanged.

Z-score averaging is a transparent working choice, not a uniquely optimal or validated constraint index. It retains distance information but is sensitive to extreme/noisy component estimates, and equal averaging allows stronger components to offset weaker ones. Comparisons with ranks, original percentages, median/IQR and median counts make methodological sensitivity visible. Correlation, small LGA samples and facility response coverage remain relevant to future model specification.

Normalization and weighting references: European Commission JRC: normalization; JRC: weighting. These support transparent construction and sensitivity checks, not causal interpretation of these particular scores.

APPENDIX · PCA SENSITIVITY

Alternative scores from the same individual demand and matched supply measures

Weighted PCA · continuous scores · modern-use models

This adapts the earlier summary's separate, standardized demand and supply PCAs to the current individual-level analysis. It changes how the two model scores are constructed; the main z-score models, heatmaps, coverage and LGA classifications remain unchanged.

Demand: each woman's own six binary indicators.Supply: her six assigned LGA–rural/urban facility measures.No new demand/supply/both/neither categories are needed for these regressions.

How the scores are constructed. Fit two separate weighted correlation PCAs, without using contraceptive use to choose the loadings. Center and standardize each input using its weighted construction sample, multiply the six standardized values by their first-component coefficients, and sum. Each woman receives her own demand PC1 and the supply PC1 corresponding to her matched facility profile. The favorable supply PC1 is multiplied by −1 for modeling so that larger values indicate weaker provision. All first-component coefficients have the expected sign in these fits.

Construction samples and weights. Demand PCA uses 1,891 FPN2 = 1 women with complete demand responses. Supply PCA uses the assigned measures for all 2,159 women in need. SURGO weights are used on both sides. Supply PCA on the woman-level records is numerically identical to PCA on the 18 distinct profiles weighted by their combined woman weights. These are not facility sampling weights. The approved popular-product fallback for Ibeju-Lekki–Urban is retained and flagged; its other five measures remain Urban-specific.

What the scores emphasize. Demand PC1 emphasizes opposition to FP, low method confidence, stigma and health beliefs. Supply PC1 emphasizes counselor availability, free provision, provider training and method offering. Three-LARC stock breadth and most-popular-product stock contribute less to the shared supply pattern. These coefficients describe variation in the inputs; they do not measure which intervention would change modern use most.

How much is retained. Demand PC1 captures 26.0% of variation across the six demand indicators; supply PC1 captures 46.8%. A single demand PC therefore summarizes only part of the information. PC2 adds 18.6% of demand variation. Keep the six-indicator models and the main equal-weight scores alongside this sensitivity.

The result supports examining reported concerns alongside services, while retaining the component heatmap to identify service weaknesses that a single PC may downweight.

Probability calculations pass direct prediction and numerical-gradient checks. LGA-clustered intervals condition on the estimated PCA scores and do not propagate PCA-construction uncertainty. Official survey EA/stratum identifiers remain unconfirmed; these are geographic cluster intervals, not a reconstruction of the full complex survey design. Supply inference remains limited by 17 LGAs and about 2.6 effective degrees of freedom for the supply coefficients.

The first PCs are exploratory compression, particularly with binary demand indicators and only 18 supply profiles. Neither PCA nor regression establishes a causal binding constraint. The main equal-weight analysis remains primary. Normalization/PCA references: R PCA documentation; JRC composite-indicator weighting guidance.
