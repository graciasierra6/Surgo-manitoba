SURGO × UNIVERSITY OF MANITOBA · SINDH

Demand, services and modern contraceptive use

Linked district-level service environments and women’s own reported concerns help identify where different kinds of support may be needed. This report follows the Lagos analysis: coverage, use and intention, joint conditions, district planning profiles, adjusted modern-use models and a PCA sensitivity.

Initial evidence: higher personal demand concern scores are associated with lower modern use. The selected supply score has an uncertain adjusted association. These findings support joint investigation of reported concerns and service gaps; they do not establish causal binding constraints.

District planning profiles · z-score average

How the map scores and thresholds are constructed
  1. Summarize each district. Demand inputs are six SURGO-weighted concern percentages among valid women in the agreed need group. Supply inputs are the six unweighted facility summaries already attached by district, with their original valid-response denominators.
  2. Standardize each component across districts. Calculate (district value − mean of valid district values) ÷ sample standard deviation. Each district contributes equally to this reference, regardless of population or respondent count. All demand inputs run higher = more concern; all raw supply inputs run higher = stronger provision.
  3. Average all six component z-scores on each side. This gives a district demand score and a district supply-readiness score. All six components are required. These are area scores for the map; the regressions use each woman's personal demand score. The map uses z-score averages, not PCA or a preliminary ranking.
  4. Compare each score with its own median. The median of 30 observed demand scores is 0.085229; flag demand strictly above it. The median of 29 complete supply-readiness scores is 0.006489; flag supply strictly below it. Exact ties are unflagged. Equivalently, a sign-reversed supply-risk score is flagged strictly above -0.006489.

Both flags → Both; demand alone → Demand only; supply alone → Supply only; neither → Neither flagged. A missing supply score gives Supply unassessed, not “Neither.” These are relative planning profiles, not causal diagnoses or validated adequacy standards. Small-sample flags do not change the scores, thresholds or categories.

Key finding. In the joint score model, the demand coefficient corresponds to an odds ratio of 0.30 (0.21–0.41) per one original average-z point. The supply risk odds ratio is 0.89 (0.73–1.10); its interval includes 1. These are conditional associations, not a causal allocation of nonuse to demand or supply.

Planning interpretation. The demand-score profile is associated with use after accounting for services and measured characteristics. The supply association remains uncertain. The heatmap identifies recorded service weaknesses for follow-up; the model does not establish that those services are adequate or that adding supply would have no effect.

Individual concerns alongside the same supply score

Model 4 enters the six personal demand indicators separately. Model 5 adds the district supply risk score. Both have the same covariates and women as Models 1–3. The supply-only estimate shown again is Model 2.

Key finding. In the six-concern joint model, opposition to FP has an odds ratio of 0.10 (0.05–0.20), and low method confidence 0.53 (0.43–0.66). The six demand indicators jointly show an association: Holm p = <0.001. Low method knowledge retains the prior display label; its questions measure confidence choosing and managing a method.

What the probability gaps mean. Model 5 predicts use for the same women with each indicator set to 1 and then to 0, retaining other observed predictors and averaging with SURGO weights. The opposition difference is -45.1 percentage points; low confidence -13.5 points. These are adjusted comparisons, not expected gains from a campaign or counseling intervention.

A result that needs care. Healthcare permission barriers have a positive adjusted association in this model: OR 1.29 (1.03–1.61). This does not mean permission barriers improve use. The cross-sectional measure is general healthcare permission, and selection, unmeasured circumstances or reverse direction can affect the relationship. Do not force every concern into an assumed negative mechanism.

Need reconstruction and its coding qualification

The supplied fpn2 variable is coded 95 on all 10,364 source rows. The authorized fpn2_derived flag excludes never having sex, SA1 codes 5/6/95/97, pregnancy, wanting a child within a year, current breastfeeding, postpartum amenorrhea, hysterectomy, definite menopause or reporting inability to become pregnant. Other missing item responses are not automatically exclusions under that upstream implementation. This is the Pakistan instrument-based reconstruction, not the broader nonuser segmentation population described in a separate memo, and not the DHS unmet-need algorithm.

Independent reconstruction agrees on every eligible weighted respondent. The chosen flag gives 3,274 women in need (32.9% weighted among 9,926 consenting positive-weight women). The later need_n variable adds 369 permanent-protection cases and is not used. SA1=95/97 do not establish sexual inactivity: they remain excluded solely to reproduce the authorized flag. Excluding only actual SA1 codes 5/6 would give 3,346 women, 72 more; this report does not silently make that change.

Weights, available sampling design and model diagnostics

Use final Normalizedweights. The available sampling design uses the recorded clusterCode field (834 clusters among consenting positive-weight women); district membership is verified to be unique within each cluster. The rb0e copy exactly matches clusterCode after single-precision rounding, which collapses some large IDs; the unrounded clusterCode is retained. Overview confidence intervals use this available cluster design after full-frame construction and domain subsetting. Original sampling strata, FPC and full design documentation remain unavailable, so a complete original-design reconstruction cannot be claimed.

Primary regression uncertainty is CR2 clustered by district to account for shared contextual supply exposure, with coefficient-specific Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. Corresponding sampling-cluster survey-GLM coefficients and intervals are exported as an inference sensitivity. Point estimates agree numerically. Both choices retain the original weights; no random effects or interactions are fitted.

APPENDIX · PCA SENSITIVITY

Alternative scores from the same personal and district inputs

Separate weighted correlation PCAs summarize the six personal demand indicators and the six assigned district supply measures. Demand PCA uses 3,096 complete women in the need group. Supply PCA uses 3,233 women with complete assigned supply values, equivalent to 29 district profiles weighted by their represented women’s summed SURGO weights. These are not facility sampling weights.

Important Sindh difference: demand PC1 assigns a negative coefficient to low method confidence. It therefore describes a contrast among concerns, not a score that necessarily increases whenever any concern increases. It is labeled “Demand pattern score (PCA).” The primary equal-weight demand risk score remains monotonic in all six concerns. PCA weights describe input variation, not causal importance for contraceptive use.

What PCA retains. Demand PC1 captures 23.1% of demand variation; supply PC1 captures 51.2%. Demand PC1 emphasizes healthcare permission and stigma, with confidence entering in the opposite direction. Supply PC1 has positive coefficients on all six favorable measures and is negated for model use.

Key finding. In the joint score model, the demand coefficient corresponds to an odds ratio of 0.79 (0.69–0.89) per one original PC1 point. The supply risk odds ratio is 0.93 (0.85–1.01); its interval includes 1. These are conditional associations, not a causal allocation of nonuse to demand or supply.

Planning interpretation. The demand-score profile is associated with use after accounting for services and measured characteristics. The supply association remains uncertain. The heatmap identifies recorded service weaknesses for follow-up; the model does not establish that those services are adequate or that adding supply would have no effect.

Individual concerns alongside the same supply score

Model 4 enters the six personal demand indicators separately. Model 5 adds the district supply risk score. Both have the same covariates and women as Models 1–3. The supply-only estimate shown again is Model 2.

Key finding. In the six-concern joint model, opposition to FP has an odds ratio of 0.10 (0.05–0.20), and low method confidence 0.53 (0.43–0.66). The six demand indicators jointly show an association: Holm p = <0.001. Low method knowledge retains the prior display label; its questions measure confidence choosing and managing a method.

What the probability gaps mean. Model 5 predicts use for the same women with each indicator set to 1 and then to 0, retaining other observed predictors and averaging with SURGO weights. The opposition difference is -45.1 percentage points; low confidence -13.3 points. These are adjusted comparisons, not expected gains from a campaign or counseling intervention.
