Composite score comparisons

These comparisons show areas with similar overall scores but different challenges. Five demand comparisons pair areas with above-average demand concerns; eight supply comparisons pair areas with below-average supply readiness. Similar overall scores can reflect different barriers; the component profile shows what the score alone conceals.

Demand concerns

1. Côte d’Ivoire · Méagui and Yamoussoukro

What this comparison shows: Both — different types of challenge and different breadth and severity

Key finding. Méagui exceeds the reference average on five components, compared with three in Yamoussoukro. Health/side-effect concerns are higher in Méagui (70.6% versus 57.5%), while low method knowledge is much higher in Yamoussoukro (76.5% versus 17.5%). The similar scores combine a broader set of above-average concerns in Méagui with a particularly large confidence-related concern in Yamoussoukro.

2. Senegal · Mbacké and Bignona

What this comparison shows: Different breadth and severity

Key finding. Mbacké has four components above average, including one marginal elevation, compared with two in Bignona. Bignona’s standout concern is low method knowledge (86.5% versus 33.3% in Mbacké). Mbacké has higher health/side-effect concerns (68.1% versus 31.7%) and opposition to family planning (34.7% versus 18.2%).

3. Lagos · Ajeromi-Ifelodun and Ifako-Ijaye

What this comparison shows: Different types of challenge

Key finding. Both LGAs have five components above average, but the mix differs. Ajeromi-Ifelodun has more social stigma (23.4% versus 7.8%) and opposition to family planning (36.0% versus 27.2%). Ifako-Ijaye has more healthcare permission barriers (24.2% versus 11.9%) and health/side-effect concerns (57.3% versus 49.0%).

4. Sindh · Kemari and Khairpur

What this comparison shows: Different types of challenge

Key finding. Kemari and Khairpur have almost identical scores, but low method knowledge is 85.2% in Kemari and 10.0% in Khairpur. Khairpur has more future-fertility concerns (89.5% versus 63.1%) and social stigma (50.3% versus 23.8%). Three components are above average in Kemari, compared with four in Khairpur.

5. Sindh · Hyderabad and Sanghar

What this comparison shows: Both — different types of challenge and different breadth and severity

Key finding. Hyderabad’s profile features high health/side-effect and future-fertility concerns (92.0% and 89.0%). Sanghar has more social stigma (65.5% versus 30.4%), low method knowledge (40.9% versus 11.0%) and opposition to family planning (25.2% versus 11.1%). Sanghar has five components above average, compared with three in Hyderabad, although its permission barriers and health concerns are only slightly above their respective averages.

Supply readiness

Every pair below has negative supply-readiness scores. Higher percentages mean better readiness in all six rows; the stockout row is shown as stock continuity = 100% − stockout burden. Bold and orange shading identify values below the component reference average.

These examples distinguish different types of weakness, broader shortfalls and profiles dominated by one large shortfall.

1. Lagos · Ibeju-Lekki and Ikorodu

What this comparison shows: Different types of challenge

Key finding. Ibeju-Lekki and Ikorodu have nearly identical readiness scores but different weaknesses. Ibeju-Lekki has lower short- and long-acting offering and LARC stock (both 51.4%, versus 74.1% in Ikorodu). Ikorodu has lower free provision (43.9% versus 70.6%) and trained-provider share (53.9% versus 72.4%). Similar totals conceal different service profiles.

2. Senegal · Matam and Goudiry

What this comparison shows: Different breadth and severity

Key finding. Matam’s main shortfall is daily provision: 29.8%, compared with the 76.2% reference average. Its free provision and training are below average by less than one percentage point each. Goudiry has four components below average, including method offering (67.6%) and daily provision (48.6%). This pair shows one pronounced shortfall in Matam alongside broader weaknesses in Goudiry.

3. Côte d’Ivoire · Abidjan and Gagnoa

What this comparison shows: Both — different types of challenge and different breadth and severity

Key finding. Abidjan has five components below average; Gagnoa has three. Gagnoa’s largest shortfall is free provision (62.8%, versus 78.3% in Abidjan), while its other two below-average components are closer to their references. Abidjan has a much lower share of facilities with a LARC in stock (49.5% versus 69.7%). Abidjan’s daily provision is only marginally below average, so five below-average components does not mean five large shortfalls.

4. Côte d’Ivoire · Bongouanou and M’Bahiakro

What this comparison shows: Different types of challenge

Key finding. Bongouanou and M’Bahiakro have almost identical scores, but their service profiles differ sharply. Bongouanou has lower LARC stock (37.0% versus 85.0%) and stock continuity (16.4% versus 42.1%). M’Bahiakro has lower free provision (62.8% versus 90.9%) and trained-provider share (16.9% versus 45.9%). The same overall readiness level combines different weaknesses.

5. Lagos · Ajeromi-Ifelodun and Ojo

What this comparison shows: Different breadth and severity

Key finding. Ajeromi-Ifelodun is below average across all six components; Ojo is below average on four, with method offering and LARC stock above average. Ojo’s daily provision is lower (17.2% versus 25.1%), while Ajeromi-Ifelodun has lower free provision (34.3% versus 42.9%) and LARC stock (68.9% versus 76.8%). Similar negative scores can reflect weaknesses across all components or a mix of strengths and shortfalls.

6. Côte d’Ivoire · Bongouanou and Tanda

What this comparison shows: Different types of challenge

Key finding. Bongouanou’s largest shortfalls are LARC stock (37.0%) and stock continuity (16.4%). Tanda has much lower free provision (62.7% versus 90.9%) and daily provision (36.0% versus 73.0%). Their similar readiness scores conceal different types of service weakness, with three components below average in Bongouanou and five in Tanda.

7. Côte d’Ivoire · Biankouma and Méagui

What this comparison shows: Different breadth and severity

Key finding. Biankouma is below average on four components, with modest-to-moderate shortfalls. Méagui has two pronounced shortfalls: method offering is 51.3% against an 80.3% reference average, and daily provision is 35.6% against 66.2%. Its other four components are above average and offset these weaknesses, bringing the overall score close to zero. The relatively favorable total therefore conceals substantial weaknesses in two services.

8. Côte d’Ivoire · Divo and Bongouanou

What this comparison shows: Different breadth and severity

Key finding. Divo has five components below average, with modest-to-moderate shortfalls. Bongouanou has two pronounced weaknesses: LARC stock is 37.0% against a 72.4% reference average, and stock continuity is 16.4% against 35.1%. Method offering is also below average, while its other three components are above average and partly offset these weaknesses. Similar overall readiness scores can therefore represent broader moderate weaknesses or fewer, deeper weaknesses balanced by strengths elsewhere.

How the supply examples were selected

All four geographies were searched using the saved whole-area scores and component values. The eight illustrated pairs have negative readiness scores, an absolute score gap no larger than 0.10 average-z points, at least 20 valid FP facilities per component in each area, and no displayed 0% or 100% component. These screening rules select readable descriptive examples; they are not statistical adequacy thresholds. The pairs illustrate different contrasts rather than a representative sample of areas. No negative-readiness area had exactly one component below average. Sindh did not supply a pair meeting the illustration screen because some component denominators were smaller. The original scores, thresholds, classifications and models are unchanged.
